Gridiron Greatness

Three studies, one game table, fifty-six seasons of NFL scores, and the arguments people have over pretzels and beer - settled with data where it can be, and left honestly open where it can't.

The questions

  • Gridiron Greatness Rating
    How good was every NFL team, really, 1970-2025? Was the 1985 Bears defense as dominant as it felt? Were the 2025 Patriots a weaker Super Bowl opponent than 14-3 suggests? Why don't the 1990s Cowboys sit at the top? A schedule-adjusted strength rating for all 1,669 team-seasons since the merger, kept separate from a score for what each team actually won. Answer in brief: the 1996 Packers are the most complete team since the merger, the '85 Bears are second, and the trophy and the strength are two different things.
  • Home Field Advantage
    How much is home field worth, and does it fade late in the year as eliminated teams' fans sell their seats? Do some fan bases travel well enough to blunt it? Answer in brief: home field has shrunk from about three points to under two, it does not fade late in the season - it grows - eliminated teams keep their edge, the 2020 no-fans season had none, and no fan base shows up in the scores.
  • Explosive Edge
    Do big plays now decide games the way turnovers do? A broadcast-booth theory tested on every play since 1999. Answer in brief: turnovers still decide games, and the exchange rate hasn't moved. But big plays decide seasons - a team's explosive-play edge repeats from one half of a year to the next, and its turnover luck doesn't.

How the three fit together

All three studies stand on the same table of every NFL game since 1970, built once and checked against published records. The rating measures teams. The home-field study measures the stadium. The big-play study measures the plays. Each one links to the others where they touch - the most explosive team-seasons are shown next to their strength ratings, and the home-field numbers use the same kind of schedule adjustment the rating does - and each one says plainly what its data can and cannot separate. The strip at the top of every page moves between them.
