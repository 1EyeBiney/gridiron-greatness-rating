Methodology

A plain-language summary. The full research specification, every settled decision, and the complete build history are in BUILD_PLAN.md on GitHub.

Scope

Every NFL regular-season and postseason game from the 1970 AFL-NFL merger through the most recently completed season. Super Bowls I-IV are excluded from the core model because the AFL and NFL didn't play each other yet, so there's no way to compare their teams' strength on a shared scale.

True Strength Rating (TSR)

TSR is a Bayesian hierarchical margin model: each game's (blowout-capped) point margin is treated as evidence about the two teams' underlying strength, with every team's rating shrunk toward the league average in proportion to how little data is available - a team's rating a few games into a season is appropriately uncertain, not a confident extreme. The model was chosen over three other candidates (a plain least-squares network rating, a logistic win/loss model, and Elo) after two independent rounds of out-of-sample testing and an audit checking its ratings against undisputed NFL history - see the repository's Phase 2 and Phase 4 documentation for the full comparison.

TSR is reported as a z-score: 0 is an average team that season, +1 is one standard deviation above average, and so on - the same units in 1972 as in 2024, which is what makes cross-era comparison meaningful.

Accomplishment (ACC)

A separate score for how successful the season was, not how good the team was: regular-season record, division title, playoff wins, and the Super Bowl result. It is never combined with True Strength - the two numbers are designed to sometimes disagree, and when they do, that disagreement is the interesting part (see the query pages for examples).

Conference Strength Index (CSI)

The estimated strength gap between the AFC and NFC in a given season, with an uncertainty range, since each team only plays a handful of interconference games. A season's CSI is considered a meaningfully imbalanced conference only when that range doesn't include zero.

Data sources

Game results 1970-1998 come from FiveThirtyEight's historical NFL dataset (MIT license); 1999-present comes from the nflverse project (CC BY 4.0). Both are unified onto one stable franchise identifier per team, correctly handling every relocation and the 1996 Browns/Ravens split. Full provenance notes are in the repository's data/raw/ directory.

What this site doesn't include yet

A comparison against historical betting-market point spreads was planned but deferred: the freely available spread datasets found all trace back to sources whose terms restrict redistribution. Two descriptive profile fields (roster quality, coach strength entering the season) need data this project hasn't acquired. Neither gap affects the True Strength Rating or Accomplishment score above.
