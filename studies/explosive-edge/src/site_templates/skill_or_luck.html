Is explosive-play differential a skill, or is it noise like turnovers?

Turnover differential is known to be mostly noise from game to game. If explosive-play differential is more persistent - a team's rate in the first half of a season predicting its rate in the second half - it is closer to a repeatable skill, and a better thing to build a team around.

Rarity or skill?

A rare event is measured with more noise than a common one, so a lower split-half correlation for turnovers could mean nothing more than that turnovers are rare. The table below tests that. For each era it takes the between-team variance of half-season differentials and subtracts the variance that counting noise alone would produce if every team's rates were fixed (Poisson for and against, over the games in a half). What is left is the share of the spread that could be repeatable, which is also the split-half correlation a noise-only world would show. Set beside the observed correlation, it says how much of the gap between the two metrics is arithmetic and how much is not.
