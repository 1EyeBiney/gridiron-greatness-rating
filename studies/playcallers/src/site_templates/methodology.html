Methodology

Unit ratings

For every regular-season game 1999-2025, each team's offensive efficiency (EPA per play, explosive-play rate, yards per play) and its opponent's same numbers are used to fit one model per season and per metric: every team gets an offense number and a defense number, and a team-game's result is explained as "your offense number, minus your opponent's defense number, plus a small home-field bump." A small ridge penalty keeps the numbers stable when a team has an unusual schedule. Offense and defense numbers are converted to z-scores within season, so 0 always means league-average that year, and defense is flipped in sign so that, like offense, higher is always better.

Assigning a play-caller

Every included team-season-unit is credited to a person: by default the titled coordinator, or the head coach where the evidence says he calls the plays himself. Of {{ facts.n_unit_seasons|n }} team-season-units considered, {{ facts.n_rated|n }} were used; {{ facts.n_excluded|n }} were excluded outright for a mid-season change, more than one named play-caller, or no titled coordinator ({{ facts.n_coordinator_gaps|n }} team-season-units have no coordinator listed at all).

Shrinkage and the variance components

A person who calls plays for several seasons gets an average rating, but that average is pulled toward the league mean by an amount that depends on how many seasons of evidence he has, and on how much teams vary year to year versus how much people vary from each other — a formula version of "regression to the mean," fit separately by unit:

Quarterback-adjusted model

A crossed model splits each offensive team-season's EPA into a caller effect and a primary-quarterback effect at the same time, with both ridge penalties chosen by leave-one-season-out cross-validation. That crossed model's error ({{ facts.qb_model_crossed_mse|f3 }}) beats a caller-only model ({{ facts.qb_model_caller_only_mse|f3 }}) but is close to a QB-only model ({{ facts.qb_model_qb_only_mse|f3 }}): knowing the quarterback predicts next season's offense about as well as knowing the caller does, so a real share of "caller signal" may be "which quarterback he happened to have."

Moves and the regression-to-the-mean baseline

For every tracked move, the unit's rating at the old team (with him, then after) and the new team (before, then with him) give an arrival and a departure change. Those are compared against a baseline built the same way but for all unit-seasons, split into terciles by the team's rating the year before the change — the fairest available comparison, since teams that hire or fire a play-caller are rarely average teams to begin with.

Coaching trees

Built only from the staff table: a mentor → protege edge exists whenever the protege was a coordinator while the mentor was head coach of the same team. A protege counts as a later head coach only if his own head-coaching seasons came after his first season under that mentor.

Sources and licences

  • Play-by-play, 1999-2025: nflverse (CC BY 4.0).
  • Coordinators, head coaches, and mid-season changes: Wikipedia team-season pages and biography pages (CC BY-SA 4.0).

Team-season pages name most coordinators. Where a page names nobody, the gap was filled from the coaching history in the coach's own Wikipedia biography, and only when that biography lists the team, the years and the title. The two sources were compared wherever both name a coordinator: they agree in {{ facts.bio_agree_n|n }} of {{ facts.bio_checked_n|n }} cases ({{ (100 * facts.bio_agree_n / facts.bio_checked_n)|f1 }} percent), and the disagreements are mostly mid-season changes. {{ facts.n_coordinator_gaps|n }} offenses and defenses still have no coordinator listed. If the same man held the job in the seasons on either side he is assumed to have held it in between, and the season is marked as a guess; otherwise the head coach is shown as a placeholder and the season is left out of the ratings.

What is excluded

Excluded from the ratings: mid-season coaching changes, team-seasons naming more than one play-caller, and team-seasons with no titled coordinator and no override naming someone else (their default is the head coach, but they are not counted). Season pages and per-play evidence beyond the team-season level are not published on this site.

Limitations

  • Play calling cannot be separated from players or head coach by scores alone. Every rating here is associated with a person and a team-season, not proven to be caused by his decisions.
  • Many play-caller assignments are unverified guesses, not Wikipedia-sourced facts — several well-known names are 100% unverified basis despite being obviously correct, and some lesser-known rows may rest on a default that turns out to be wrong.
  • Defensive results are noisier than offensive ones; defensive rankings should be read with wider margins in mind.
  • Small samples dominate the person-level lists. A 3-season rating and its standard error should not be read as a settled verdict.
  • Mid-season changes were excluded outright, so some real, sometimes informative in-season coaching changes are invisible to every table on this site.
  • The underlying metrics (EPA, explosive rate, yards per play, and the points that follow) reflect much more than play calling — offensive line, secondary, injuries, turnover luck, and opposing talent all flow through the same box score. The opponent adjustment controls for schedule strength, not for any of this.
  • The moves analysis is a small, noisy sample set against baselines that are themselves imprecise.

Everything on this site is offered as "associated with," never "caused."
