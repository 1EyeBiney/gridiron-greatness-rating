Which fan bases travel?

Every franchise's home/road swing - and why it is the only version of this question that scores can answer.

What is being measured

For each franchise, take every home game and ask how many points it beat expectation by; do the same for every road game, from the team's own point of view. The difference between the two averages is the team's home/road swing. For an ordinary team the swing is twice the league home-field advantage: it gets the edge at home and gives it up on the road. The league-average swing over 1970-2025 is {{ facts.swing_league|f1 }} points.

A team whose fans travel well enough to neutralise road crowds should show a small swing - it gives up less on the road. But a small swing is also what a team with a quiet home crowd would show, and from scores alone the two can't be separated. The methodology page explains why. What the table below can say is whether any franchise's home/road split is unusual at all. The "z" column is the swing's distance from the league average in standard errors; with {{ facts.n_franchises }} franchises, one or two beyond ±2 is what chance produces.

What the swing is not

The swing is a franchise's home results minus its road results, both against expectation, pooled across every season in the window. It is a fair way to ask whether any team's home/road split is unusual. It is not an estimate of what a stadium is worth. Denver's {{ facts.den_swing|f1 }}-point swing (z = {{ facts.den_z|f2 }}) says the Broncos have played about {{ (facts.den_swing / 2)|f1 }} points better than expected at home and about the same worse on the road, across two stadiums and fifty-five seasons; it does not say Mile High adds {{ facts.den_swing|f1 }} points, or any number.

A venue study would be built differently: one effect per stadium and era rather than one per franchise, estimated alongside team strength with small samples pulled toward the league average, allowing for travel distance, rest, altitude, weather and roof, and checked for whether a stadium's estimated edge persists into seasons it was not fit on. It would report a range for each venue and test whether the venues differ at all before ranking them. Even then it would answer "which venues carry an extra edge," not "which fans cause it"; the second needs attendance, occupancy or visiting-fan measures this project does not have, and attendance itself follows winning.

How much could still be hiding here? Each swing is measured to about ±{{ facts.swing_se_median|f1 }} points, so a franchise whose fans were worth a point a game on the road would sit only about a standard error below the league line, and would not be picked out by this table. "Nothing detectable" is the finding; "nothing" is not.
