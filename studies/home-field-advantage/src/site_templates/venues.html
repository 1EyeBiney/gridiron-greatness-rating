The season with no fans

In 2020 the NFL played a full schedule with stadiums empty or nearly so for most of the year. Home-field advantage that season was {{ pm(facts.hfa_2020, facts.se_2020) }} points, and it was back at {{ facts.hfa_2021_25|f1 }} over 2021-2025. That is consistent with the crowd being a large part of home field. It is not proof of it, for two reasons the first version of this page left out. First, home field had already fallen to {{ pm(facts.hfa_2019, facts.se_2019) }} in 2019, the year before anyone stayed home, so a single near-zero season is not by itself unusual. Second, 2020 changed more than the seats: the preseason was cancelled, practice was restricted, and attendance limits varied by city and by month rather than being switched off league-wide. A proper test would compare games under different attendance limits within 2020, allowing for venue and team, against the seasons around it. This study has not done that, and reports the 2020 figure as suggestive.

Division games

Home field is worth less against a division opponent than against anyone else, in every part of the season ({{ facts.div_all|f1 }} points against {{ facts.nondiv_all|f1 }} overall). Familiarity is the usual explanation: teams that play twice a year know each other's stadiums, crowds, and habits, and that erodes part of what makes a road trip hard. The late-season rise is present in both groups.
